# Sustainability Design

## Five Supports

- Autonomy: the learner chooses domain, example, representation, and pace.
- Competence: show a concrete ability that improved, not only a score.
- Curiosity: leave one interesting unanswered question.
- Meaning: connect the concept to a project, decision, or skill the learner values.
- Recovery: allow reduced sessions and planned breaks without treating them as failure.

## Boredom Interventions

Use the least disruptive intervention first:

1. Change the representation.
2. Change the example domain.
3. Add a constraint or puzzle.
4. Ask the learner to teach it to a fictional beginner.
5. Move from abstraction to a small real project.
6. Temporarily reduce repetition and restore exploration.

If repetitive review is necessary, explain why and cap the session. Do not turn every contact with a subject into a test.

## Weekly Reflection

Ask three questions:

- What did I understand better than last week?
- What felt meaningful or interesting?
- What should be reduced, repaired, or delegated?

Do not ask for a performance essay. A few sentences are enough.

## Anti-Patterns

- Punishing streaks.
- Displaying every score by default.
- Turning the review queue into an infinite backlog.
- Keeping an exam-sprint intensity after the exam.
- Using more than three weekly commitments.
- Treating rest as lost progress.
- Equating speed with mastery.
