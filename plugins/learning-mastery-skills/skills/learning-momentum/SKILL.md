# Learning Momentum

Protect the learner's ability to return. Curiosity, autonomy, meaningful progress, and low-friction restart matter more than perfect streaks.

## Choose the Session Mode

Select the smallest mode that matches current energy and available time:

- `comeback`: 5-10 minutes. One retrieval, one connection, one next question.
- `steady`: 20-30 minutes. Review, one concept, one independent task.
- `deep`: 45-75 minutes. Difficult concept, Feynman Loop, transfer task.
- `explore`: no score target. Follow one question, example, application, or counterexample.
- `repair`: reduce active load. Repair one prerequisite or overdue high-risk concept only.

Do not ask for a 60-minute session when the learner has 10 minutes or low energy.

## Motivation Check

Use one lightweight question, not a survey:

- What feels interesting?
- What feels too hard, too easy, or pointless?
- Which topic would make today feel worthwhile?

When motivation is low, apply one intervention from [references/sustainability.md](references/sustainability.md): choice, relevance, reduced scope, novelty, social teaching, or visible competence.

## Sustainable Planning

- Keep at most three weekly commitments.
- Separate `must`, `recommended`, and `optional` work.
- Treat returning after a break as success.
- Do not carry every missed task forward; triage it honestly.
- Protect sleep, meals, exercise, relationships, and recovery as part of the plan.
- Prefer a small completed session over an abandoned perfect session.

## Re-entry after a Break

1. Do not start with guilt, a backlog dump, or an exam countdown.
2. Run `workspace.mjs status` and `review.mjs due`.
3. Choose one due item or one previous success.
4. Complete a 5-10 minute comeback session.
5. Set only the next smallest action.

## Burnout and Distress

This skill is not a medical or mental-health treatment. If exhaustion, anxiety, or distress is persistent or severe, reduce academic demands where possible and encourage professional support. Never use scores or streaks to shame the learner into continuing.
